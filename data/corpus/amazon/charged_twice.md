# Amazon — Multiple Charges for the Same Order

## Authorization holds vs actual charges
When you place an order, your bank reserves funds until the transaction processes or the authorization expires. Some banks display authorizations as "pending" or "processing" charges. What looks like a duplicate charge is often an authorization hold, not an actual second charge. If you cancelled an order before it shipped, an authorization may still be visible on your statement but is not an actual charge.

## Multiple shipments
Orders may be split into multiple shipments — or multiple orders — which can result in multiple charges. The total amount charged should add up to the amount displayed when the order was placed.

## When you change an order
If you change an order before it ships, Amazon may ask your bank for a new authorization and inform the bank that the previous authorization is no longer necessary. The bank then cancels it and releases the funds within **5–7 days**.

## Getting help
For policies on authorizations, contact the issuing bank. If you believe you were genuinely double-charged, contact Amazon Customer Service through your account, or ask your bank to confirm whether the charges are pending authorizations or actual charges.
